Abstract
1
Streaming large language model (LLM) services can expose response-length metadata even
2
when payloads are encrypted. We evaluate a fixed T5 implementation with its recorded
3
candidate-selection rule through 6600 reconstructions from validated application-layer
4
length traces. For 300 aligned prompts per model, five archived-reference cohorts yield
5
mean term-frequency cosine scores (ϕ) of 0.482–0.535; two additional archived cohorts have
6
malformed word boundaries. Newly generated TinyLlama and Phi-3.5-mini responses
7
with verified detokenization yield means of 0.435 and 0.504. Legacy rendering of their
8
saved token IDs reproduces all 600 corresponding archived references and length vectors.
9
Changing both the trace and scoring reference increases mean ϕ by 0.356 and 0.383 under
10
matched reconstruction settings. In two-model pilots, three of six partial-ablation contrasts
11
remain significant after Holm correction; neither cross-prompt contrast is conclusive. Five
12
framing transformations reduce frozen-reconstructor mean scores by 36.7–88.7%. A five-
13
seed study with a local empirical maximum-a-posteriori (MAP) length inverse recovers
14
much of the loss under bucketing and batching. Reconstruction scopes and retained
15
encoder coverage differ across these experiments. The findings quantify lexical behavior of
16
an instrumented pipeline and demonstrate the importance of validating both observations
17
and reference rendering. They do not measure semantic disclosure or demonstrate passive
18
extraction from encrypted transport.
19
Keywords: large language models; information leakage; side-channel analysis; server-sent
20
events; traffic analysis; token-length traces; text reconstruction; privacy

1. Introduction
22
Interactive large language model (LLM) services stream partial responses to reduce
23
latency. One implementation uses Server-Sent Events (SSE), with generated text carried in
24
application events. Encryption hides the event contents but not necessarily the shape of the
25
communication. If application-event boundaries or correlated record sizes are observable,
26
a sequence of lengths can reveal properties of the underlying tokens.
27
This paper separates reconstruction from a known length trace from extraction of that
28
trace from encrypted traffic. The laboratory observable is the ordered UTF-8 byte length
29
of each token fragment emitted by an instrumented service, before transport processing.
30
A validated log oracle supplies these lengths and preserves newline bytes and empty
31
fragments. This abstraction removes segmentation by HTTP and Transport Layer Security
32
(TLS), buffering, retransmission, and multiplexing from the experiment. The historical

Building on Weiss et al., who reconstructed streamed responses from token-length

41

The study addresses three research questions:
44
RQ1:
Do ordered logged lengths improve lexical scores over controls that shuffle
45
lengths, replace them with a constant, or use another prompt’s trace?
46
RQ2:
How do scores from validated length traces differ across seven archived victim-
47
model cohorts, and how do separate experiments with verified reference decoding
48
inform the two diagnostic series?
49
RQ3:
How do bucketing, fixed-width units, batching, and randomized padding affect
50
the evaluated pipeline, protected-payload size, and observable-unit count, and
51
how does a prompt-disjoint maximum-a-posteriori (MAP) inverse change its
52
scores?
53
The contribution is an evaluation with explicit input provenance: 2100 reconstructions
54
from validated archived lengths, 600 additional reconstructions of newly generated and
55
correctly assembled responses, two-model pilots of partial trace ablations and five framing
56
configurations, and a five-seed MAP-inverse evaluation. The pilots and MAP study use
57
different reconstruction scopes, so their baselines are not interchangeable. Full input
58
vectors, decoder inputs, random seeds, generated texts, and independent metric checks
59
are retained in the accompanying artifact package. The source project is hosted at https:
60

61

2. Related Work
63
Side-channel attacks infer sensitive information from indirect observables such as tim-
64
ing and message size. Classical timing attacks showed that implementation-level leakage
65
can defeat cryptographic protection without recovering plaintext directly [2]. Encrypted
66
network applications likewise leak through packet sizes and timing. Keystroke inference,
67
web-application traffic analysis, and website fingerprinting demonstrate that encryption
68
does not hide communication shape [3–6].
69
Traffic-analysis defenses transform that shape through padding, morphing, batching,
70
or cover traffic. Traffic morphing attempts to make one traffic class resemble another, while
71
subsequent studies show that efficient countermeasures may retain exploitable structure
72
or impose substantial overhead [7,8]. For this reason, defense effectiveness should be
73
evaluated by reporting both the leakage that remains and the resulting bandwidth and
74
latency overhead, rather than by treating padding as either fully protective or ineffective.
75
For generative AI, Weiss et al. introduced T5-based sequence-to-text inversion and, in
76
their first-segment evaluation, reported 27% of reconstructions above an embedding-cosine
77
threshold of 0.9 and approximately 53% above 0.5 [1]. Their cosine uses MiniLM sentence
78
embeddings; its thresholds and first-segment scope are not directly comparable to our
79
term-frequency cosine and full-response LR@0.5. Li et al. subsequently studied API outputs
80
that merge several tokens into one observable unit and showed that aggregation does
81
not remove the side channel [9]. Whisper Leak instead infers prompt topics from packet-

size and timing patterns in encrypted LLM traffic [10]. Those studies address stronger
83
deployment-facing threat models. The present work narrows the observation layer to
84
ask a different question: how a fixed reconstructor transfers across victim models once a
85

86

87
The reconstruction pipeline uses T5-style text-to-text transformers [11]. Evaluation
88

89

90

3. Materials and Methods
92
3.1. Threat Model and Objective
93
We evaluate a length-only reconstructor for an instrumented LLM service. The ob-
94
servation is the ordered byte length of each emitted token fragment; the attacker receives
95
neither the generating prompt, token identities, nor victim-model internals. The evaluator
96
uses instrumented logs to verify these lengths and to obtain reference text, but supplies
97
only the lengths to the reconstructor. This application-layer oracle does not recover event
98
boundaries from TLS, HTTP/2, or QUIC traffic. The archived-reference cohort and the
99
newly generated two-model cohort are kept separate because verified byte lengths do not
100
themselves establish ordinary detokenization of historical outputs.
101
The objective is to characterize lexical reconstruction in this controlled apparatus and
102
the response of a fixed reconstructor to length transformations. The experiments capture
103
per-token traces, reconstruct text from length sequences, and compare the reconstructions
104
with the stored emitted responses. Prompts are used to generate those responses and are
105
not supplied to the reconstructor. Figure 1 shows the intended information flow.
106
3.2. Information-Theoretic Interpretation
107
Let R denote the stored emitted response and let X = (L1, . . . , LT) denote its ordered
108
token-payload length trace in the idealized laboratory model. When a defense is applied,
109
X is transformed into the observable trace Z = g(X, N), where N is independent trans-
110
formation randomness. The attacker produces bR = A(Z, U) using independent decoder
111
randomness U. Conditional on a fixed fitted attacker, these relationships form the following
112
Markov chain:
113
R −→X −→Z −→bR.
(1)
114
According to the data-processing inequality,
115
I(R; bR) ≤I(R; Z) ≤I(R; X).
(2)
116
Neither processing stage increases mutual information about R under these assumptions.
117
The empirical controls are partial trace ablations: they preserve response-dependent fea-
118
tures such as trace length, the length histogram, or mean length. A score advantage over
119
an ablation indicates sensitivity to the altered features, but does not isolate all trace infor-
120
mation from the decoder’s language prior. The lexical score ϕ does not estimate mutual
121
information, and its reduction for a fixed attacker does not measure defended-channel
122
capacity.
123
For a single-segment reconstruction, let E(Z) denote the deterministic encoder input
124
after inversion (if used), segmentation, serialization and truncation. Then the chain refines
125
to R →X →Z →E(Z) →bR, and I(R; E(Z)) ≤I(R; Z). The complete trace Z may retain
126
variation that never reaches the decoder. This distinction matters for pad_32: the audited
127
single-call encoder inputs are constant within each evaluated condition (Section 4.6). Multi-

3.5. Reconstruction Pipeline
185
The inversion pipeline follows the sequence-to-text procedure introduced by Weiss
186
et al. [1]. It first splits a length vector at observations equal to one and merges short
187
chunks using a ten-unit minimum, with an additional list-boundary heuristic. These
188
are heuristic segments rather than verified sentences. The first segment is decoded us-
189
ing royweiss1/T5_FirstSentences; later segments use royweiss1/T5_MiddleSentences,
190
conditioned on the immediately preceding selected segment. T5 generation uses stochastic
191
sampling with top-k = 50, temperature 1 and a two-token no-repeat n-gram constraint.
192
The executed selector sums normalized transition scores over all returned generation steps,
193
including PAD positions after a candidate’s first end-of-sequence (EOS) token; the chosen
194
text supplies the next segment’s context.
195
These scores are obtained after the sampling constraints, including top-k and the
196
no-repeat rule; their sum is not an unconstrained model likelihood. The implementation
197
does not mask post-EOS steps, which can assign nonfinite scores to a completed candidate
198
and alter selection. A bounded replay reproduced all 24 selected first-segment predictions,
199
but ranking only through the first EOS changed the selected text in 18 cases (Appendix C).
200
All three-candidate results in this paper retain the original PAD-inclusive selector. They
201
describe this specific implementation and do not estimate the performance of an EOS-
202
masked selector. The one-candidate MAP experiment bypasses candidate ranking.
203
The pinned model cards identify these checkpoints as the T5-Large fine-tunes used
204
by Weiss et al., trained on the first answers from UltraChat’s “Questions About the World”
205
subset. The source procedure trains separate models for first segments and subsequent
206
segments, with preceding-segment text providing context for the latter [1]. We verify the
207

3.7. Evaluation Metrics
256
Reconstruction quality was computed against the complete reference response R using
257
four lexical criteria. The normalized Levenshtein value is ed_norm = d(R, bR)/|R|, using
258
reference character length as the denominator; it can exceed 1 when the prediction is
259
much longer than the reference [12]. ROUGE-1 precision and ROUGE-L precision measure
260
unigram and longest-common-subsequence overlap [13]. The primary metric ϕ is cosine
261
similarity between unweighted, case-normalized term-frequency vectors [21], with terms
262
defined by [A-Za-z0-9']+. All reported metrics were recomputed from complete saved
263
text, using independent edit-distance and subsequence implementations checked against
264
dynamic programming. The MAP experiment scores the first heuristic segment’s prediction
265
against the entire response, not against an annotated first sentence.
266
The study also reports lexical recovery rate LR@0.5, the proportion of samples with
267
ϕ > 0.5. This threshold is a descriptive operating point, not a calibrated success criterion.
268
Neither ϕ nor LR@0.5 establishes factual, semantic, or operationally useful reconstruction.
269
3.8. Trace-Information Controls
270
We compared four inputs on the same topic-balanced set of 30 prompts for Qwen2.5-
271
1.5B-Instruct and Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct using direct logged lengths from the 96-token
272
pilot: (i) the original trace; (ii) its lengths randomly permuted within the response; (iii)
273
a constant sequence equal to its rounded mean length; and (iv) another prompt’s trace
274
cycled to the target trace’s number of positions. These are partial trace ablations. Shuffling
275
preserves the response’s length histogram and number of positions, the constant condition
276
preserves its mean-derived value and number of positions, and the cross-prompt condition
277
preserves the target trace’s number of positions while replacing the length pattern. Thus
278
each condition retains some response-dependent information. The comparisons probe
279
sensitivity to order, variation, and alignment; their residual scores cannot be assigned
280
wholly to the language prior. The control experiment reconstructs up to three heuristic
281
segments against the full stored response and saves the actual segment counts and encoder
282
inputs. These comparisons are not estimates of mutual information.
283
3.9. Statistical Analysis
284
Because the same 300 prompts were issued to every model, comparisons of the retained
285
scores used prompt-aligned observations. For the archived main and verified-reference
286
cohorts, percentile-bootstrap 95% confidence intervals [22] use B = 10,000 resamples from a
287
generator initialized with seed 20260706; the pinned aggregator consumes this generator in
288
its recorded model, metric and contrast order. The pilot and MAP intervals use separately
289

312

313

315

316

317

318

319
•
bucket_8: replace ℓt by 8⌈ℓt/8⌉, independently and online for every event.
320
•
pad_32: replace each token-length observation by a constant 32-byte protected unit.
321

322
within the unit.
323
•
batch_2 and batch_4: sum the lengths of consecutive groups of two or four tokens

325

327

331

332

3.11. Repeated Decoder Seeds and Adaptive Attacker
345
The primary seven-model table uses one assembled reconstruction per sample, select-
346
ing candidates from three stochastic samples per segment. Decoder-seed uncertainty and
347
a defense-aware attacker were evaluated using direct logged lengths from the 512-token
348

349

350
calibration set with no identical test prompt. All 30 test scenarios nevertheless occur in 120
351
calibration rows under the other four wording templates. The test uses the first wording
352
template throughout. Thus the split is exact-prompt-disjoint, but not scenario-disjoint. An
353
EOS-complete-only split would have left 137 (Qwen) and 167 (Llama) calibration traces,
354

355
24 (Qwen) and 21 (Llama) were EOS-complete.
356
Reconstruction was repeated for five decoder seed indices, 20260706–20260710. For
357

358

359

360

361

362

363
as independent prompts. These reconstructions use one stochastic T5 sample for the first
364

365

366

367

368

369
test prompt. The inverse selects a modal length conditional on an observed value, or a
370

371

372
global MAP estimate of the response’s entire length sequence. This is a calibrated length
373
inverse, not end-to-end T5 retraining. Baseline traces are scored with the frozen reconstruc-
374
tor only. Each of the five transformations is scored with both the frozen reconstructor and
375

376

5. Discussion
543
5.1. Evidence of Trace-Dependent Recovery
544
The strongest control evidence concerns replacing the trace by a constant: both mod-
545
els lose lexical score, and shuffling also reduces Qwen’s score after correction. Neither
546
cross-prompt contrast is conclusive, and Llama’s shuffled contrast is inconclusive after
547
Holm correction. Thus the experiments support sensitivity to selected trace features, not
548
a universal advantage of the target trace over every partial ablation. Controls preserve
549
response-dependent quantities such as length, mean or histogram and can change seg-
550
mentation. Their residual scores cannot be assigned entirely to the decoder’s language
551
prior. Estimating information-theoretic leakage would require a defined response variable,
552
repeated sampling and a suitable estimator.
553
5.2. Limits of the Cross-Model Comparison
554
Exact logged byte lengths remove the line-collector ambiguity from the reported
555
reconstruction inputs. Reference quality remains a distinct issue: archived TinyLlama and
556
Phi-3.5-mini fragments have malformed word boundaries, whereas the new two-model
557
cohorts pass explicit full-sequence decoding checks. For fixed newly recorded token IDs,
558
legacy decoding reproduces the archived inputs and references exactly, while full-sequence
559
decoding restores word-boundary spaces. With reconstruction settings and per-sample
560
seeds held constant, both resulting score increases have paired scenario-bootstrap intervals
561
excluding zero. This effect jointly includes the changed length trace and changed scoring
562
reference; neither contribution is isolated. The archived low scores therefore cannot support
563
a general conclusion of poor reconstructor transfer to these two models.
564
The five other archived-reference series support conditional comparisons of the fixed
565
reconstructor on their emitted texts, not an intrinsic model-vulnerability ranking. Historical
566
token IDs and exact victim revisions are unavailable, while tokenizer vocabulary, response
567
style, refusal behavior and compatibility with T5 training data can differ. The matched
568
two-model rendering experiment is consequently reported separately from those five series.
569
The synthetic corpus also contains only 60 scenarios, and the MAP test and calibration sets
570
share those scenarios despite exact-prompt disjointness.
571
5.3. Practical Exploitability
572
For the largest mean in the archived-reference cohort, LR@0.5 is 65.33% and ROUGE-1
573
precision is 0.2921. Neither quantity is a disclosure rate: lexical overlap does not verify an
574
entity, proposition or actionable answer. No semantic annotation or task-specific disclosure
575
evaluation was performed. Practical harm might involve recovered phrases, entities,
576
refusals or topic cues, but the present metrics do not quantify those outcomes. Deployment-
577
facing conclusions additionally require extraction of observables from encrypted traffic and
578
validation under transport buffering and multiplexing.
579
Reconstruction performance, transport observability, and mitigation performance
580
require separate evidence. The MAP inverse recovered score losses on bucketing and
581
batching within the evaluated pipeline, but it also changes segmentation and the amount
582
of input retained under the 80-token encoder limit. The study therefore measures the
583
combined effect of the length transform, inverse, preprocessing, and fixed decoder. For
584
pad_32, the complete observable trace retains its number of positions, but the actual retained
585
encoder input is constant within each condition. Its positive lexical scores are consequently
586
not evidence that this decoder exploits response-specific length variation. This finding
587
concerns E(Z) and does not establish absence of information in Z for other attackers. A
588
comparison that fixes observation coverage across conditions would help isolate these
589
https://doi.org/10.3390/electronics1010000


Version November 1, 2026 submitted to Electronics
19 of 24
mechanisms. End-to-end training on defended traces and disclosure-oriented human or
590
task evaluations remain separate extensions.
591
5.4. Limitations
592
The three-candidate experiments retain a selector that includes PAD scores after EOS.
593
The bounded replay establishes actual selection changes under EOS masking, but does
594
not estimate their frequency across the campaign or their effect on full-response scores.
595
Later segments condition on the selected text, so corrected full-response results require a
596
separate end-to-end campaign with the revised selector and retained candidates. Current
597
score rankings, confidence intervals, rendering contrasts and defense comparisons are
598
conditional on the original implementation. The one-candidate MAP results are unaffected
599
by this selection mechanism.
600
The observation is an instrumented log oracle. No packet capture or experiment with
601
TLS, HTTP/2 or QUIC demonstrates that its token boundaries are available to a passive
602
observer. The two historical reference-format diagnostics remain in the archived cohort for
603
traceability, and the other archived references lack original token-ID provenance. Newly
604
generated references are verified for TinyLlama and Phi-3.5-mini, but their configurations
605
are not certified replicas of the historical victim executions.
606
The primary cohorts use one assembled reconstruction per prompt with three stochas-
607
tic samples per segment. Decoder-seed uncertainty is evaluated separately for two models
608
and 30 prompts; five seeds are not five independent prompt samples. Scenario bootstrap
609
accounts for grouping within the 60 represented scenarios, without establishing general-
610
ization beyond the synthetic corpus. MAP calibration and test inputs are disjoint as exact
611
prompt strings, not as scenarios. Pilot decoder seeds are condition-specific, and their single
612
realizations cannot separate all stochastic variation from transformation effects.
613
The main archived responses reach the token limit in 34.3–100% of cases; both historical
614
and new Phi-3.5-mini cohorts lack EOS-complete responses. Reconstruction processes all
615
heuristic segments in the primary and new-reference cohorts, up to three in the 96-token
616
pilots, and one in MAP. All use the full stored response as the scoring reference. Transform-
617
dependent segmentation and the 80-token encoder limit prevent treating these scopes
618
as matched information budgets. The MAP inverse is not end-to-end retraining, and
619
payload overhead excludes transport syntax, buffering delay and latency. Finally, the
620
historical Gemma cohort uses a different victim-generation implementation from the other
621
six archived cohorts.
622
5.5. Ethical Considerations
623
The study used synthetic prompts and locally generated responses; it did not intercept
624
user traffic or process identifiable personal data. The artifact release is intended to support
625
defensive evaluation of streaming implementations. Deployment testing should use au-
626
thorized systems, controlled accounts, and coordinated disclosure when a service-specific
627
weakness is found.
628
6. Conclusions
629
The completed experiments comprise 6600 T5 reconstructions and 600 newly generated
630
victim responses. Reconstruction from validated logged lengths yields mean lexical scores
631
of 0.482–0.535 for five archived-reference cohorts. For TinyLlama and Phi-3.5-mini, verified
632
full-sequence decoding yields means of 0.435 and 0.504. Legacy rendering of the newly
633
saved token IDs exactly reproduces the corresponding archived references and length
634
vectors. Under matched T5 settings and seeds, changing the rendering of both the input
635
trace and scoring reference increases mean ϕ by 0.3560 and 0.3831. This computational
636

comparison identifies observation and reference construction as consequential parts of
637
the evaluation; it does not separate their individual contributions or establish an intrinsic
638

639
The partial-ablation controls support sensitivity to selected trace features: three of six
640
contrasts remain significant after Holm correction, while neither cross-prompt contrast is
641
conclusive. In the 96-token pilots, all ten model–transformation contrasts show significant
642

643

Appendix B.2. Decoder, Transformation and Bootstrap Seeds
718
Define H(a1, . . . , am) by converting each argument with Python’s str, joining the
719

720

721
with 0x7fffffff. This is the artifact function stable_seed; it is independent of Python’s
722
randomized hash().
723
The immutable main and verified-reference inventories store each exact seed in the
724
reconstruction_seed field. The matched renderings use the same recorded seed for each
725
prompt. For the two pilots, the decoder seed is
726
H(secondary-pilot-decoder-v1, 20260706, label, i, suite, condition),
727
where i is the recorded prompt index and the suite is trace_control or defense_pilot.
728

729
is H(secondary-pilot-trace-v1, 20260706, label, i, condition); shuffling instead uses
730

731

732

733

734
across decoder repeats and attacker modes.

735

736

737

738
